Home

Your home screen is a camera near you.

Open the app and you see the sky from a real camera close to where you are. The card under it reads the nearest weather station and tells you what is coming and when: “rain reaches you around 3:40,” or “dry for the next hour.”

Type in your address, call it Home or Cabin or whatever you want, and switch between there and where you are standing with one tap.

Radar

Radar you can scrub.

Drag the slider back to see where the storm came from and forward to see where it is going. The next hour comes from the radar too, so the home card can say “rain starts in 20 minutes” instead of “60% chance today.”

Turn cameras on over the radar and tap one under the storm to see what is falling.

One map. The same controls for every layer.

Stations

Readings from the station down the street.

The map sorts stations by what they are: airports, backcountry sites, and backyard stations that people run at home. In the canyons and small towns the backyard station is usually the only one there, and it is the one that matches what you see out the window.

Tap a station for its reading and its forecast. Pin the closest one and check it from the Pinned tab.

Lists

The pass, the trailhead and your parents’ town, in one list.

Each list shows a live camera, the current reading and an alert when something changes. Make one for the drive to the cabin and another for the places you climb.

Sign in with Apple or Google and your lists come with you to a new phone. Signing in is optional. The app works the same without it.

Themes

Eight themes. Day and night.

Pick one and the whole app changes with it. Most have a day and a night version, and Auto follows the sun where you are. Camera images and warning colors stay the same in every theme. Themes are part of Pro.

Anywhere

Works in any town in the US.

Search a place and you get the same screens. Here is Asheville, North Carolina: 44 cameras within ten miles, a weather station under a mile away, the forecast, and the French Broad River gauge with its history.

How it works

Fort Collins, Colorado

Made here, by one person.

I’m Ryan. I drive the Poudre, check the snow, and got tired of a forecast for town that says nothing about the canyon, so I built this.

The app reads NOAA and the National Weather Service, USGS river gauges, USDA SNOTEL snowpack, EPA AirNow, state DOT cameras and NIFC fire data, and shows you where every number came from.

Your location is used to fetch your weather and for nothing else. If you sign in, we keep your email and a backup of your pins, and you can delete both from inside the app.
