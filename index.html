Home

Your home screen is a camera near you.

Open the app and you see the sky from a real camera close to where you are. The card under it reads the nearest weather station, often a backyard one a few blocks away, and tells you what is coming and when: “rain reaches you around 3:40,” or “dry for the next hour.”

Type in your address, call it Home or Cabin or whatever you want, and switch between there and where you are standing with one tap.

Cameras

More than 30,000 cameras, credited and live.

Mountain passes, harbors, airports, river gauges, national parks and research stations, in all 50 states. Tap a camera on the map or search it by name: Cameron Pass on CO‑14, Statter Harbor in Juneau, the NOAA station at the South Pole.

Every camera names who runs it and refreshes on the schedule they publish. Nothing is recorded or archived, and a camera owner can slow theirs down or take it off with one email. Every camera is free to look at and always will be. How we treat cameras.

Lists

The pass, the trailhead and your parents’ town, in one list.

Pin the spots you check and put them in a list. Each list shows a live camera and the current reading for every spot in it. Make one for the drive to the cabin and another for the places you climb. Send a list to a friend with a link.

Pins and lists are part of Pro, and every new install gets them for 14 days. Sign in with Apple or Google and your lists come with you to a new phone. Signing in is optional. The app works the same without it.

Radar

Radar you can scrub.

Drag the slider back to see where the storm came from and forward to see where it is going. The next hour comes from the radar too, so the home card can say “rain starts in 20 minutes” instead of “60% chance today.”

The same map carries cameras, weather stations, snow depth at SNOTEL sites and river flow at USGS gauges. Turn cameras on over the radar and tap one under the storm to see what is falling. Search is on the map too. Radar and cameras are free. Stations, snow sites and river gauges are part of Pro.

Customize

Looks that move with the weather.

Loom draws twenty-six threads under glass cards. They ripple with the wind at your spot, pull tighter in a gust, and their color follows the temperature, ice blue through gold to rose. Journey to Pluto flies a pixel ship over speed lines that run with the wind and fires on every big gust. Eight Classics round out the Locker, in day or night colors. Daylight is free. The rest come with Pro.

iPhone Duo · coming in an update

Opens up on iPhone Duo.

Unfold the phone and the camera gets a whole pane. Conditions, the next hours and the map sit beside it instead of under it, and you can put two cameras side by side: the pass and the town. Fold it and the same screen is back in your pocket.
